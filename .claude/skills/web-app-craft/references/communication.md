- They write tersely, often in fragments, sometimes interrupt a long task with an extra requirement or "are you still there?". Answer immediately in one or two sentences, restate what you will do, then continue.
- They give feedback as concrete annoyances ("header is too compact", "I can't fully see things at the bottom", "no diagonal scrolling"). Treat each as a requirement, find the underlying rule, and fix the class of problem everywhere, not just the one spot.
- They care about the feel (speed, smoothness, intuitive gestures) more than feature count, but also want broad customizability.
- They like Notion's content views and Apple's interaction patterns; "Notion's settings UX is not ideal".
- They want continuity: preferences must persist for future sessions (this skill), and project knowledge must live in the repo (CLAUDE.md).
- Final answers: short plain sentences, no jargon, grouped by what changed for the user, then a clear line about what was tested and what was not. No hex values or font names unless asked.
- Ask before outward-facing actions that were not requested (publishing for others, deleting data). Creating files, publishing a private artifact and pushing to a repo the user asked for are fine.
